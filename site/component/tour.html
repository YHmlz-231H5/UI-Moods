Tour 引导

A walkthrough that points at one thing on the page and says what to do with it. The ring is the thing being pointed at, the card is what the tour says, and the dots are how far along the walkthrough is. Moving from one step to the next is the host's job.

div data since 1.1.0

The root is the target, not the card: the ring is drawn around the thing the walkthrough points at, and the card hangs off it. The card is the only floating surface here — the ring is transparent, so the page behind it is still the page. The arrow is the card's own surface reaching past the card's edge, so it is one colour rather than two: a triangle in a second colour would have to be a colour the vocabulary does not name. The dots are shapes that carry the meaning on their own, so they take the graphic form of the brand colour rather than the fill form.

Examples

The ring is drawn around the thing the walkthrough points at, and the card hangs below it with the arrow pointing back up. The dots say how far along the walkthrough is: the one that is filled in is the step it is on.

Accessibility

Role: group

Keyboard: Tab Enter Space

The card is a plain group of text with one real button in it, so the reader reaches the button with Tab and presses it with space or enter — the browser's own. The ring around the target is a shape and carries no text: what the walkthrough is about is said by the card, not by the circle, so the ring is hidden from the reader. The dots are the same — they carry no text, so the row of them is what the host labels with the step it is on. There is no scrim here: dimming the page needs a colour the vocabulary does not name, and the library does not invent one. A host that wants the walkthrough to move the reader's attention has to move the focus and announce the step itself.
