Watermark 水印

A layer of the same word, repeated across a box, to say that what is underneath is a copy. The word is real text in the markup, tiled by the browser rather than baked into a picture.

div other since 1.1.0

The tile is real text, and the repetition is in the markup: a gradient paints bands and not glyphs, and a word baked into an SVG would need a font the library does not ship — and could not be translated. So the host writes the tiles out and the layer flows them into a lattice, which is the same thing a picture would have done, except that the word stays a word. The word is painted in the placeholder role, which is the only role the contract has for text that is deliberately faint; a role of its own would be better, and is worth adding to the contract.

Accessibility

The watermark is decoration and says nothing the content does not already say, so the layer is hidden from the reader with aria-hidden and takes no pointer events. The word is real text rather than a picture, so it is still selectable by a reader who wants to copy it — and it can be translated, which a baked-in picture could not be. Put the word in the content too, if the reader has to know it: a watermark nobody can read is not a notice.
