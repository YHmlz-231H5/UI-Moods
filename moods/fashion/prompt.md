# Mood: fashion

Fashion is a printed magazine page, not a screen. The layout is stark
white, the palette stripped to ink black and true white, and the only volume
comes from Didot and Bodoni — high-contrast serif headlines set enormous at
fifty-two pixels, with hairline rules and wide margins that let the page
breathe. There are no shadows, no glows, no blur: nothing floats and nothing
rounds. Blocks, cards, buttons and inputs all meet the page on hard
zero-pixel edges, and the single pill in the system, the badge, is the one
small concession to the human hand. Weight carries the hierarchy instead of
colour, with asymmetry doing the work a centred logo would normally do.
Motion is unhurried at four tenths of a second on a long, literary easing
curve, so a change of state feels like a page turning. The mood feels
composed, expensive and editorial.

## Keywords

`fashion`, `editorial`, `didot`, `asymmetric`

## When to use

- Lookbooks, collections and seasonal campaign pages
- Brand storefronts, product detail pages and editorial spreads

## When to avoid

- Operational dashboards that need compact density and clear affordances
- Dark or playful products where monochrome austerity misreads as cold
